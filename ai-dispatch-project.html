An operations command-center case study built around 30,000 synthetic mobility service events. I built this case study to find where automated dispatch needs human help, understand why it happens, and decide what an operations team should do next.

Synthetic portfolio data. No HONK, customer, or provider records are used. The model uses operating concepts I worked with, including automated dispatch, manual intervention, provider assignment, SLA, ETA and ATA, partner type, cost, and NPS. It does not use proprietary data. The cost and ETA examples below are also modeled for this case study.

How I measure it. Automated Dispatch Rate is the share of jobs assigned without a dispatcher stepping in. Affiliate Utilization is the share handled outside the preferred provider network. TTFD measures the time from the service request to provider assignment. SLA Attainment is the share of jobs that meet the service target. All dashboard values are modeled for this portfolio project.

Root-cause framework

Adding providers is not always the answer.

Network coverage

Provider density, rates, distance and acceptance can create geographic failure even when overall supply looks healthy.

Phoenix: 23.5% manual intervention
41.6% affiliate

Peak capacity

A mature market can still fail during demand spikes. LA Friday evenings averaged about 14.0 minutes TTFD with roughly 40.4% SLA attainment in the modeled scenario.

Capability

In the Las Vegas scenario, about 39% of tow exceptions come from capability. I would recruit for the equipment and capabilities the market is missing instead of simply adding more providers.

Partner economics

I use preferred versus affiliate mix as an operating signal. If manual intervention and affiliate use are both high, I would review rates, coverage, acceptance, and provider density.

Customer and fleet experience

ETA accuracy

84%Arrivals within 10 minutes of ETA

Finding a provider quickly is only part of the job. I would also track whether the provider arrives when expected because ETA accuracy affects downtime, customer confidence, and escalation volume.

A fast assignment with a bad ETA is still a poor service experience.

What I would do next

Turn the analysis into an operating plan.

Phoenix

Build more preferred provider coverage in the weak areas. Review declines by rate and distance, then target recruiting where affiliate use is highest.

Los Angeles

Focus capacity on Friday evenings. Adjust routing rules and provider availability for the hours where manual intervention and TTFD climb.

Las Vegas

Recruit for the tow equipment and capabilities that are missing. More providers will not help if they cannot handle the job type.

30 day check. I would compare automated dispatch rate, TTFD, SLA attainment, affiliate utilization, ETA accuracy, and cost per event against the starting point. If the numbers do not move, I would change the intervention instead of assuming the first plan worked.

Executive takeaway

Find the problem. Understand the cause. Fix it. Measure the result.

I start with the hotspot, look at automation and provider mix, and work through the reason jobs are failing. Then I choose the fix and watch what happens to TTFD, SLA, cost, and customer experience.
