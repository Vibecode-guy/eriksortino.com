Executive briefing · what this command center demonstrates


A fictional Los Angeles autonomous fleet needs coordinated roadside response, safe recovery, and measurable partner performance. This tabletop exercise demonstrates how an operations leader can triage exceptions, assign capability-matched providers, monitor response SLAs, and evaluate staging investments.




Operational decision
Which compatible recovery partner should respond, and when should an incident be escalated? Use the dispatch workbench to compare eligible partners and progress the response.


Capacity decision
Should an additional response crew stage near LAX/Westside, the Valley, or San Pedro/Harbor? Compare modeled p90 response, queue time, SLA attainment and incremental cost.


What success looks like
Faster simulated on-scene response, fewer modeled SLA misses, transparent recovery spending, and clear operational tradeoffs. Metrics are scenario outputs, not historical business results.


What is intentionally excluded
Live vehicle feeds, actual traffic routing, real partner prices, customer data, commercial trip utilization, and real-world safety authorization.




Guided 60-second demonstration


  1. Choose an available AV and generate an immobilization or flatbed event.
  2. Select the new incident in the queue and assign an eligible fictional response provider.
  3. Advance through en route, on scene and completed; observe SLA and cost updates.
  4. Adjust fleet size, incident demand and budget in Scenario planning; explain the recommended crew-staging tradeoff.


The workflow is manually driven and deterministic. There is no live dispatch integration or automated safety decision.




Methodology & limitations. Synthetic deterministic workflow, not a dispatch recommendation for real vehicles. Simulated travel uses straight-line distance and a fixed effective speed; no traffic, permits, service contracts, real SLAs, real rates, safety clearance, or operational telemetry. The clock advances only when you press controls. Response time is incident creation to on-scene; p90 uses nearest-rank among completed incidents with an on-scene timestamp. Averages and p90 display “—” before any completed incidents. No actual company or partner data.
