Data provenance

City of Los Angeles · MyLA311

Publisher: City of Los Angeles Information Technology Agency. Dataset: MyLA311 Service Request Data 2024. The City describes it as service requests submitted through 3-1-1, call centers, email, mobile apps, websites and other sources. The published dataset has 34 columns and includes creation/update dates, owner, request type, status, source, assignment, service/closure dates and geographic fields.

Open official source ↗

This page can query the City's public Socrata API for a live analytical snapshot. If the API is unavailable, methodology remains visible and no synthetic results are substituted.

Executive decision brief

From demand signals to better service

Analyze 1.44 million 2024 service requests to identify demand concentration, reporting patterns and questions for operational follow-up. The figures below are live City data, not claimed improvements.

Priority investigation

Start with the highest-volume request types, then compare ZIP-level patterns, closure timing, service mix and available capacity before changing staffing or routing.

Success measures

Track category-adjusted closure time, aging requests, assignment handoffs and data completeness against a documented baseline.

Operational application — provider network planning: In roadside assistance, incident-demand heatmaps can identify markets to investigate for towing-provider recruitment. Compare hotspots with existing tow-provider locations, service areas, availability, acceptance rates and ETAs; prioritize underserved coverage gaps, contract qualified providers, then monitor acceptance and response performance. This reflects the type of network-expansion decision process I used at HONK, not a claim that MyLA311 contains towing incidents or HONK provider data.

Illustrative operations decision

Central LA dispatch & service coverage scenario

Decision question: If dispatch is coordinated from Central Los Angeles, where should field resources be positioned to serve nearby demand reliably? A central dispatch desk is a coordination point—not proof that a truck, crew or service partner is physically available there.

01 · Identify demand

Use the 2024 MyLA311 ZIP heatmap and ZIP-level request counts to identify high-volume service areas. Compare request types before treating all reports as equivalent work.

02 · Test a Central LA hub

Model Central LA as a hypothetical dispatch origin, supported by smaller proposed field teams in the San Fernando Valley and South LA. Treat South LA and LAX / Westchester as primary coverage, with San Pedro / Harbor as extended coverage where delays are anticipated. Validate real routes, traffic, capacity and response targets before committing to SLAs.

03 · Validate coverage

Obtain actual resource locations, working hours, skills, available capacity, travel-time estimates, response SLAs and service-area boundaries. This dataset does not provide those inputs.

04 · Decide and measure

Prioritize additional coverage where verified demand and response gaps overlap. Pilot changes, then monitor response times, acceptance/completion rates and unmet demand before scaling.

Scenario status: Planning framework only; no provider placements, dispatch travel times, capacity shortfalls or optimal hub locations are calculated or claimed. Executive decision brief

From observations to management actions

1 · Validate the bottleneck

Compare median and 90th-percentile closure durations, open backlog and category mix before interpreting averages. Avoid treating a long closure as proof of a staffing shortage.

2 · Target the operating response

Test whether assignment timing, workload concentration, routing or vendor capacity explains the delay. Pilot a narrowly scoped change with a comparison period.

3 · Measure the outcome

Track completed volume, backlog age, closure-duration distribution and service quality. Compare against baseline and account for seasonality and category mix.

4 · Evaluate tradeoffs

Use the scenario lab to size potential labor capacity, then validate realizability and implementation cost before making financial claims.

Transformation logic

What I did to the source data

-- Example analytical SQL after ingestion SELECT request_type, owner, status, request_source, created_date, closed_date, EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (closed_date - created_date))/3600 AS resolution_hours, CASE WHEN closed_date IS NULL THEN 'Open' WHEN closed_date - created_date <= INTERVAL '24 hours' THEN '<= 24 hours' WHEN closed_date - created_date <= INTERVAL '72 hours' THEN '24-72 hours' ELSE '> 72 hours' END AS resolution_band FROM myla311 WHERE created_date IS NOT NULL;
Source fieldDerived / QA treatment
CreatedDateParse timestamp; derive month, weekday and hour.
ClosedDateParse timestamp; calculate resolution duration only when valid.
StatusStandardize labels; separate open/pending/closed/canceled states.
RequestTypeTrim/normalize categories; inspect long-tail values.
Owner / AssignToProfile responsibility and missing assignment.
Latitude / LongitudeType-check, reject null/implausible coordinates before mapping.
RequestSourceNormalize intake channels for channel-mix analysis.
Operating questions

The dashboard is not the conclusion.

Demand

Which request types and geographies generate disproportionate volume? Is demand seasonal or concentrated by day/channel?

Service delivery

Which categories remain open longest? Are differences explained by request type, ownership, assignment, or intake channel?

Data quality

Where are closure dates, assignments or coordinates incomplete enough to distort a performance metric?

Management action

Which bottlenecks deserve staffing, routing, self-service, process redesign or deeper root-cause analysis?
